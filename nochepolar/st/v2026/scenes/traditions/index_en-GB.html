United States

In the United States, stockings are hung on Christmas Eve. The legend goes that St Nicholas heard of a family in need. To surprise them, he climbed down their chimney and placed precious bags of gold in hanging stockings. From this point, St Nicholas was known as Santa Claus, giver of gifts.

Mexico

In Mexico, children of all ages celebrate Christmas by breaking piñatas. Adults and children have fun by wearing a blindfold, spinning around and swinging a stick to break open the star-shaped hanging pots. When the piñata is smashed open, nuts, fruit and sweets spill out for everyone to enjoy.

Guatemala

Guatemalans celebrate Christmas with fun and dancing. They wear hats called Puritina, eat Guatemalan-style tamales, light firecrackers at midnight and dance the night away with various styles of dancing.

Canada

Canadian children usually celebrate a snowy, white Christmas. They bundle up in their scarves and toques, a Canadian woollen hat, to play in the snow, ice skate and toboggan! On Christmas Eve, they leave milk and cookies by the fireplace for Santa (and sometimes a carrot for his reindeer).

Argentina

In Argentina, Christmas begins promptly at midnight. Many families kick off the holiday by hosting festive celebrations in their homes, opening gifts with their loved ones and lighting fireworks in the early hours of the morning.

Brazil

Christmas celebrations start near midnight, usually with big family dinners, gift opening and the celebration of the 'Missa do Galo' (the rooster's mass) in churches. Despite the warm tropical summer weather, themes of winter and snow are not uncommon.

Finland

Finnish people believe that Father Christmas, 'Joulupukki', lives in the northern part of the country – Korvatunturi, to be exact. If you visit, you might find his secret workshop, where gnomes are hard at work. Or you might find reindeer roaming in pine tree forests.

Iceland

At Christmas time in Iceland, families give gifts of warm clothing to each member of their household. According to legend, there is a frightening Christmas Cat who gobbles up anyone not equipped for the cold and wintery weather. Families work together to ensure that nobody will 'go to the Christmas Cat'.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, merrymakers of all ages enjoy Christmas crackers on the table at dinner. Crackers are tubes wrapped in colourful paper, with pull tabs on each end. When you pull the tabs, the tube bursts with a loud snap! Inside are paper hats, toys, jokes and prizes to enjoy during Christmas night.

Ghana

Christmas in Ghana includes the whole family. Adults dress up in costumes and hand out sweets around the neighbourhood. Children decorate their homes with paper ornaments and drawings they make at school.

France

In France, children traditionally put their shoes out next to the fireplace in anticipation of Père Noël's arrival. If children have been good throughout the year, Père Noël will fill the shoes with gifts.

Germany

In nearly every German town, Christmas markets open in the town square. Townspeople gather to hear brass bands and enjoy traditional fare from the region. They eat gingerbread hearts, sugar-roasted almonds, sweets of all kinds and drink mulled wine (Glühwein) or apple cider.

Greece

In Greece, friendly but troublesome creatures called 'kallikántzari' live most of the year in the centre of the earth. But at Christmas, they love to hide in people's houses, frightening people, playing tricks or devouring Christmas foods. Greeks perform rituals to keep these hobgoblins away.

Kyrgyzstan

In Kyrgyzstan, Ayaz Ata (Grandfather Frost) comes at midnight on New Year's Eve and leaves presents under the New Year tree. When the clock strikes midnight, families throughout the country light fireworks to celebrate this beautiful occasion. The cities become as bright as day.

South Korea

In South Korea, Christmas is a national holiday. It's also known for being a romantic holiday. Many couples go on dates in the city, where streets and shops are trimmed with dazzling lights.

Russia

Russian children decorate a New Year's tree, instead of a Christmas tree. Children wait with longing for Grandfather Frost who travels with his granddaughter Snowmaiden, delivering presents across the land. Grandfather Frost lives deep in the woods in Veliky Ustyug, in northern Russia.

Lithuania

In Lithuania, traditional Christmas ornaments are made out of straws or hay. They're woven into geometric shapes, like stars, snowflakes or bells. Many families also use straw to decorate the dinner table for a big meal called Kūčios.

The Philippines

The Philippines is known for having the world's longest Christmas season. Four months are considered Christmas months: September, October, November, December. Christmas carols can often be heard as early as the beginning of September.

Australia

Australia's Southern Hemisphere location means it's summer time in December. Instead of sleigh bells and cocoa, many Australians head to the beach to celebrate the holidays with a prawn lunch and games of cricket.

New Zealand

Because Christmas is in the summer in New Zealand, friends and family gather round a barbecue. Typical Christmas meals include roast lamb, sometimes cooked in a 'hangi' – an underground pit or earth oven. A favourite dessert is pavlova: meringue covered in fresh fruit like strawberries and kiwifruit.
